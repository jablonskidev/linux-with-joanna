If I'm going to understand how Linux really works, then I'm going to need to be able to explain it.

When I make content in my [professional life](https://github.com/jablonskidev), I determine what goals each piece of content needs to achieve and how to best achieve those goals.
That's not what I'll be doing here.
This blog will be more exploratory, personal, and off-the-cuff.

Since I plan to get more personal, here are a few things about me that tie in with how I approach Linux:

- **I have Soviet design sensibilities.**
  I used to sleep on a mattress on the floor that I called a "Soviet Murphy bed" because I would lean it up against the wall when I needed room to exercise.
  The fewer moving parts something has, the more I like it.
  My favourite tool is a rock.
  I appreciate Debian.

- **I have minimalist and monk-like tendencies.**
  My life is more improved by removing things than adding things.
  I replaced that mattress on the floor with an exercise mat and now sleep on that.
  I'm open to a little less comfort and convenience if the tradeoffs are worth it.
  I dream of a life that can be packed up in three bags.
  I've dabbled in Arch and distros that are designed to be tiny.

- **I get distracted by black boxes.**
  I don't feel that I really understand how something works until I can explain every layer of abstraction.
  In this blog, I'll start by explaining the basics of how Linux works, but I'm looking forward to properly digging into [Linux Programming Interface](https://nostarch.com/tlpi) and [Linux Kernel Development](https://www.amazon.com/Linux-Kernel-Development-Robert-Love/dp/0672329468/).

You're very welcome to join me on this journey. Let's figure out how Linux works together.
